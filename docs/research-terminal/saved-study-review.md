# Saved-study review and descriptive routing

Saved-study comparisons must distinguish the existence of a completed calculation from the completeness and historical availability of its source evidence. A saved result alone does not verify its arithmetic, validate a hypothesis, establish historical tradability, or demonstrate a replay match.

## Request interpretation

Research bookkeeping words such as “investigation” and requests for an offline replay ZIP must not turn a descriptive calculation into a trading strategy. Explicit exclusions such as “do not substitute stock-trading data” do not authorize that source. Actual backtest, causal, predictive and strategy requests retain their execution guards.

A request to inspect saved studies takes precedence over earlier portfolio questions. Previous chat remains context, but prior portfolio specifications cannot automatically trigger fresh backtests or replace the requested records. Legacy replay and optimization dispatch require a resolved legacy request.

The revenue-minus-operating-costs prompt can save a descriptive blocked design when compatible numeric observations are absent. Its unresolved inputs and date choices remain explicit. A blocked protocol is immutable: importing data does not automatically make that saved graph executable. A new resolved protocol must identify the actual observations and design choices.

## Evidence contract

`research_audit_saved_studies` inspects exact saved experiment IDs or the latest saved experiments for exact dataset IDs. Without IDs it audits at most four distinct pinned datasets from the latest 500 experiment metadata rows. This is bounded discovery, not a census or an automatic match to every name in the question. Unselected and missing candidates are disclosed.

The audit checks recorded bodies, input hashes, original engine hashes and dataset pins. It returns:

- Provider receipts, normalized records, selected signals and collection partition status as distinct quantities.
- Dataset and calculation exclusions, their field origins and omitted-counter counts.
- Recorded statistics, portfolio rules, cooldowns and actual result windows.
- Publication assumptions and unverified historical ownership, with explicit limits on before-news and look-ahead claims.
- Source-semantic conflicts in preserved legacy caveats, including stock-filing language in procurement studies.
- Explicit statements that no calculation was replayed and no new backtest was executed.

Unknown fields remain unknown. Matching partition counts mean count reconciliation only within the declared collection scope. Original archived caveats are preserved; the audit does not rewrite history or certify their assertions. Database consistency checks do not authenticate source truth or replace a complete archive-closure audit.

The tool uses the normal owner registry lifecycle and then performs read-only inspection. Controlled notebook memory, scheduled prompts and web-only sourcing do not grant access. Larger bounded comparisons receive enough model context to preserve their ledgers. Answer cache identity version 8 prevents reuse of answers generated under the earlier routing/reporting behavior.

## Answer verification

The answer cites the audit for coverage, timing, exclusion and recorded-result claims. Narrow semantic checks flag the demonstrated overclaims as well as requested but omitted evidence. They feed the existing revision pass alongside numeric and citation checks.

If a saved-study narrative still has grounding problems, the terminal displays a deterministic cited evidence report with selection and claim limits. It does not label that report as an independently validated study. Percentage-point differences are supported by the numeric grounding checker, separately from basis points.

These checks target explicit, testable failures. They are not a general natural-language proof system or a scientific-validity certificate. The broader corpus, publication-timing validation and general methodological-execution gaps remain separate work.
